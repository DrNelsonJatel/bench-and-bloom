Poured slowly over cold milk, the syrup goes in as a ruby ribbon and turns violet blue on the way
down. It is worth pouring in front of someone the first time.

This is the iced half of the [Lavender Milk](/recipes/lavender-milk), and it works for the same
reason. Our syrup is acidic, milk is not, and butterfly pea flower reads the difference and changes
colour to match. Cold milk holds the layers a little longer than warm milk does, so you get to watch
the whole thing happen before it settles.

Go slowly with the pour. Tip the glass and let the syrup run down the inside if you want the ribbon
to hold. Stir once at the end, or leave it and let each sip find its own colour.

Whole dairy gives the truest violet. Plant milks will get there, paler, and are best poured slower.

Start from a bottle of [Bench & Bloom](/syrup), or a batch of
[lavender simple syrup](/recipes/how-to-make-lavender-simple-syrup).
