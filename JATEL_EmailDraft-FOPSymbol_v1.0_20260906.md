Hello,

I am requesting the **Compendium of Nutrition Symbol Formats** and the accompanying **directory of
symbol dimensions**, for a prepackaged food label going to print this season.

**About the product**

Bench & Bloom is a small farm-based producer on the Naramata Bench in British Columbia. The product is
a **lavender flavouring syrup, 250 mL**, in a clear glass Boston round bottle, sold in Canada with a
bilingual label. This is our first production run.

**Our determination that the symbol applies**

- Reference amount **30 mL**, Table of Reference Amounts item **U.15**, "syrups used as ingredients"
- Serving of stated size: **2 tbsp (30 mL)**
- Sugars: **26 g per 30 mL = 26% DV**
- Because the reference amount is **≤ 30 mL**, the applicable threshold is **10% DV** rather than 15%

The product exceeds that by roughly 2.6 times, so we understand the **"High in sugars / Élevé en
sucres"** symbol to be required. For completeness: were U.14 (60 mL) the correct reference amount
instead, the product would carry 51 g of sugars per reference amount, or 51% DV against the 15%
threshold, so the symbol would be required either way.

We do not consider the Division 18 exemption for sweetening agents sold as such to apply, as this is a
compounded botanical flavouring syrup rather than a sweetening agent.

**What I am requesting**

1. The **bilingual "High in sugars / Élevé en sucres" symbol in EPS format**
2. The **directory of symbol dimensions**, so we can select the correct size for our package

**Sizing information, in case it helps you point us to the right row**

The container is cylindrical. Calculating the principal display surface under the *Consumer Packaging
and Labelling Regulations* s. 2, paragraph (c), at 40% of the side area excluding top and bottom, we
get approximately:

- **60 cm²** counting the straight label panel only, or
- **91 to 94 cm²** counting the full side including the shoulder and neck

Both fall within the **> 30 cm² to ≤ 100 cm²** range, so we believe that is our size row. **If you are
able to confirm which of those two bases is correct for a bottle of this shape, that would be very
helpful**, since our figure sits close to the 100 cm² boundary.

**One secondary question, if it falls within your unit's scope**

Can you confirm that **U.15 rather than U.14** is the appropriate reference amount for a syrup
intended for cocktails, sparkling wine and coffee? It does not change whether the symbol is required,
but it does determine our declared serving size and therefore the Nutrition Facts table.

## Notes for you, not for the email

**Why this is the critical path.** The symbol must sit in the **upper half of the principal display
panel** with a clear buffer, and it cannot be redrawn by hand from a picture. Until the EPS and the
dimensions arrive, the front panel cannot be laid out, and until the front panel is laid out the
label cannot be redrawn. Everything else on the blocker list is either an instrument reading you can
take yourself or a CFIA tool session you can book.

**What is deliberately not in the email.** No pH, no Brix, no batch data. None of it bears on the
symbol, and the Brix in particular is still unmeasured, so there is no reason to put an unverified
number in front of a regulator.

**Framing.** The determination is stated as ours, not asked as a question, because the request is for
artwork and it is our responsibility to establish that the symbol applies. The two questions at the
end are framed as optional courtesies so they cannot become a reason to delay sending the files.
